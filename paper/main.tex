\title{Facts, Trust, and Boundary Primitives:\\
A Foundation Model for Real-World Agent Work}
\author{Keren Dong\\Kungfu Origin Technology Limited\\\href{mailto:keren.dong@kungfu.link}{keren.dong@kungfu.link}}
\date{Draft: July 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agents increasingly participate in work that outlives a conversation and
crosses repositories, runtimes, releases, people, and machines. At these
contact surfaces, coordination that once survived in human memory becomes a
systems problem. This paper proposes a foundation model for that problem:
facts must not drift; trust must start from facts; cooperation must start from
trusted value. It then derives three procedures for declared perspective,
human--agent primitive discovery, and autonomous discovery grounded in causal
experience. The central systems hypothesis is that a strong primitive often
emerges at a pressured boundary where participants previously depended on
implicit translation, handoff, attribution, or responsibility. We examine
three current cases: Xinfa Atlas between project reality and human/agent
understanding, Kungfu Episode between agent action and real-world result, and
Buildchain Release Passport between reviewed source and released artifacts.
We show how Project Cut composes spatial, causal, material, and release facts
without collapsing their authorities. Boundary pressure is treated as a
diagnostic rather than proof; deletion, fuse, held-out, counterfactual, and
independent-verifier tests remain necessary. The contribution is a falsifiable
systems design and an implementation-backed research program, not a claim that
the cases are already universal or historically important.
\end{abstract}

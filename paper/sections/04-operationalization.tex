\section{Derived Procedures for Primitive Discovery}
\label{sec:operationalization}

The foundation says what must remain true. The derived procedures say how to
act when perspective and discovery become load-bearing.

\subsection{KFD-4: declare the observer}

Real-world work combines facts from machines, agents, repositories, and
external systems. A mixed-source timeline is not an absolute view from
nowhere. KFD-4 requires a perspective-bearing view to declare the observer,
accepted sources and ranges, projection policy, causal constraints,
deterministic tie-breakers, freshness, and degraded state.

This does not make facts relative. Source-local records, provenance, causal
links, hashes, and receipts remain load-bearing. The projection is relative to
an observer and evidence cut. Known causality must dominate presentation
policy; missing evidence should degrade the view rather than be hidden behind
wall-clock sorting. The procedure follows distributed-systems results that
reject a single physical clock as sufficient causal authority
\cite{lamport1978time}.

KFD-4 is necessary for primitive discovery because the pressure itself is
observed from somewhere. Two investigations may honestly reach different
conclusions when their cuts, values, or operating boundaries differ. The
observer declaration makes that disagreement inspectable rather than
silently universalizing one view.

\subsection{KFD-5: couple grounded judgment and scalable reasoning}

KFD-5 defines the current executable discovery form. Grounded judgment
contributes contact with consequences, value, and the ability to notice that a
recurring burden matters. Scalable reasoning contributes search, comparison,
reconstruction, formalization, counterexample generation, and verification.
Humans and agents commonly supply these functions respectively, but the
functions are not permanent species boundaries.

A candidate record preserves the following chain:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item ground a recurring burden in a declared evidence cut;
  \item bind sources, observers, gaps, and responsibility;
  \item generate candidates without privileging the first coherent name;
  \item compare prior art, narrower abstractions, and doing nothing;
  \item demand minimum identity, boundary, authority, lifecycle, and
        operations;
  \item run deletion and fuse tests;
  \item declare evidence that would reject, narrow, subsume, or reopen the
        candidate;
  \item dogfood it under load; and
  \item record accepted, provisional, rejected, or subsumed as valid outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

The \emph{deletion test} asks whether removing the candidate forces
participants to reconstruct the same missing coordination. The \emph{fuse
test} asks whether mechanisms that previously appeared separate become one
more economical system when the object exists. Neither test proves a
primitive; both expose whether the name carries independent explanatory and
operational load.

\subsection{Boundary pressure as a diagnostic}

When the implicit-boundary pattern is present, the discovery record can add:

\begin{equation}
B_X = (L, H, \Delta P, \Phi, M_X, N),
\end{equation}

where $L$ names the contact sides, $H$ the previous implicit mechanism,
$\Delta P$ the pressure change, $\Phi$ recurring failures, $M_X$ the
candidate's mediation claim, and $N$ a narrower internal-object alternative.

The hypothesis gains support when the candidate makes translation, handoff,
attribution, authority, or responsibility stable and inspectable; deletion
recreates the old burden; and the narrower alternative fails to explain the
same evidence at lower cost. It loses support when an internal model, policy,
label, or local abstraction removes the friction without exporting a new
identity.

Boundary pressure is optional and non-exclusive. A valid primitive may be
discovered through another grounded mechanism, and a heavily pressured
boundary may require no new primitive. This asymmetry protects the framework
from becoming a universal naming machine.

\subsection{KFD-6: autonomous discovery without self-certification}

The draft KFD-6 asks whether sufficiently rich causal experience can let an
agent perform more of the discovery step without a human translating every
reality pressure. An experience corpus may include observations, choices,
actions, dependencies, outcomes, costs, failures, retries, overrides, and
receipts connected by causal links. It remains a projection of reality, not
reality itself.

An autonomous loop must declare corpus and observer cuts, missing evidence,
selection and instrumentation bias, the boundary hypothesis, held-out
evaluation evidence, bounded intervention authority, and a separate promotion
authority. The generator cannot be its sole verifier, and generated examples
cannot satisfy the evidence gate. The intended loop is:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Atlas}_n \rightarrow \text{action} \rightarrow \text{Episodes}
\rightarrow \text{boundary pressure} \rightarrow \text{candidate}
\rightarrow \mathrm{Atlas}_{n+1}.
\end{equation}

KFD-6 remains draft until a real implementation beats retrieval or
summarization baselines, rejects attractive false candidates, transfers a
candidate across contexts, preserves disconfirming evidence, and leaves an
auditable path from corpus cut to promotion decision. Failure is evidence
against activation, not a reason to weaken the gate.

\subsection{Promotion rule}

Discovery and promotion are distinct authorities. A candidate may validate a
schema, explain prior cases, or be proposed by an agent without becoming
load-bearing. Promotion should be proportional to consequence and supported
by independent evidence. This separation applies KFD-2 to the discovery
procedure itself and prevents a coherent loop from manufacturing its own
trust.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Not a metaphysical claim about agents}

The model treats agents as intelligent participants for product design
purposes. It does not require a metaphysical claim about consciousness or
personhood. The practical claim is enough: if a product depends on an agent's
reasoning, interpretation, explanation, and recovery behavior, then the
product should give that participant inspectable facts, trusted value, stable
choice, and explainable constraints.

\subsection{Not anti-control}

KFD-3 can be misread as rejecting strong constraints. It does not. It rejects
hidden pressure and unreviewable control. Sandboxes, permission gates,
revocation, policy enforcement, and refusals remain necessary. The requirement
is that constraints be fact-bound, explainable, auditable, and proportionate.

\subsection{Not complete without implementation evidence}

The current model is compact enough to be attractive, but compactness can hide
hard engineering work. Non-drifting facts require registers, schemas, hashes,
locks, release gates, compatibility boundaries, and review practices.
Inspectable trust requires evidence capture and residual-risk vocabulary.
Trusted value requires product surfaces that agents and humans can actually
use. The model should be judged by whether those mechanisms work in real
systems.

\subsection{Risk of over-extension}

The foundation can become too broad if every desirable property is folded into
it. This paper intentionally keeps the foundation to three layers. Later KFDs,
such as observer-declared timelines, should be treated as practice guidelines
or procedures built on the foundation rather than expansions of the foundation
itself.

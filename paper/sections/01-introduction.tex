\section{Introduction}

Software systems have long crossed boundaries: source becomes an artifact,
events become state, and local observations become shared decisions. Much of
the coordination at those boundaries has remained implicit. A maintainer
remembers why a release is trustworthy. A worker reconstructs which facts a
handoff meant. A user infers whether a successful command implies durability.
When the participants are few and the environment is stable, this invisible
work can be absorbed by human memory and convention.

Agents change the pressure on those boundaries. They can act across many
repositories, tools, machines, and time spans, but they do not inherit the
unstated context that made an old process work. Humans, meanwhile, cannot
continuously reconstruct every action, artifact, fact source, and residual
risk produced at agent speed. The resulting problem is not only how to expose
an API to an agent. It is how to build a shared work world in which humans and
agents can recover what happened, decide what to trust, and cooperate without
hidden control.

This paper starts from a compact foundation published as the first three Kung
Fu Decisions (KFDs) \cite{kfd2026alpha25}:

\begin{quote}
KFD-1: facts must not drift.\\
KFD-2: trust must start from facts.\\
KFD-3: cooperation must start from trusted value.
\end{quote}

The three statements form an ordered model rather than an independent list.
Non-drifting facts make claims inspectable. Inspectable claims make value
trustable. Trusted value makes durable cooperation possible. Later KFDs derive
procedures from that foundation: KFD-4 requires perspective-bearing timelines
to declare their observer; KFD-5 joins grounded judgment with scalable
reasoning to discover primitives; and the draft KFD-6 asks whether agents can
perform more of that discovery from causal experience without certifying their
own output.

The central systems hypothesis developed here is:

\begin{quote}
A strong primitive often appears at a contact surface that was handled
implicitly until a new participant or new scale made its coordination cost
load-bearing.
\end{quote}

Such a primitive does not merely describe one side of a system. It mediates a
boundary: translation, handoff, attribution, authority, responsibility, or
recovery becomes a stable object that software can identify and inspect. This
idea is related to boundary objects in sociology \cite{star1989boundary}, but
the focus here is an engineering gate for promoting a candidate into a
first-class identity with lifecycle, authority, operations, and falsifiers.

We study three current cases. A \emph{Xinfa Atlas} sits between project reality
and human/agent understanding. A \emph{Kungfu Episode} sits between agent
action and real-world result. A \emph{Buildchain Release Passport} sits
between reviewed source/build activity and the artifact world that users
receive. Each previously depended on a human reconstructing an implicit
boundary. Each now has, at a different maturity level, a named identity and
machine-readable evidence surface.

This paper makes five contributions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item an ordered foundation for facts, trust, and cooperation in real-world
        agent work;
  \item a derived primitive-discovery procedure in which boundary pressure is
        a strong but non-exclusive diagnostic;
  \item a comparative analysis of Atlas, Episode, and Release Passport as
        boundary-primitive candidates;
  \item a composition model using Project Cut to keep material, epistemic,
        causal, and release authorities separate while binding them; and
  \item a falsifiable evaluation program based on deletion, fuse, recovery,
        held-out, counterfactual, and independent-verifier tests.
\end{itemize}

The claims are deliberately bounded. The paper does not claim that every
boundary needs a primitive, that the three cases are universally applicable,
or that their historical importance is established. KFD-6 remains a draft
experiment. The contribution is a systems design with inspectable
implementation evidence and explicit conditions under which its stronger
claims should be rejected.
